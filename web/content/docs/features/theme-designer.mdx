The theme designer lets you build verse overlays the same way a graphic
designer would — by composing on a canvas — and then sends the result to
NDI exactly as designed.

![Theme designer with the theme library on the left, the 1920×1080 canvas in the centre with the verse text selected, and its text properties on the right](/docs/screenshots/theme-designer-canvas.png)

*The theme designer. Pick a theme on the left, click an element on the canvas (here the verse text) and edit its font, size, spacing, alignment and colour on the right; the Background and Layout tabs cover the rest of the slide. **Click to expand.***

## What you can compose

- **Backgrounds**: solid colors, multi-stop gradients, still images, or
  fully transparent (the four background types in `BroadcastTheme`).
- **Typography**: choose any installed system font, set size, weight,
  letter-spacing, line-height, and alignment.
- **Effects**: drop shadows, outlines/strokes, fills, and blur.
- **Layout**: drag elements to move them and pull their handles to resize
  them; the canvas reflects the exact NDI output dimensions. Boxes snap to
  the frame's edges and middle and to each other (hold Alt to place
  freely), and the Grid button adds a grid to snap to. Arrow keys nudge the
  selected box (Shift for 10px); scroll pans and Ctrl+scroll zooms.
  Dragging or resizing the reference or verse frame detaches them into
  **free positioning** mode, where each block has its own
  X/Y/width/height (also editable as percentages in the Layout tab). Keep
  them stacked and the classic above/below/inline flow applies instead.
- **Camera panel**: a live camera in a box of its own, with rounded
  corners, a border and a shadow, placed and sized anywhere (Camera tab).
- **Design-tool controls**: padding, corner radius and drop shadows are
  set the way Figma does it. Padding is left and right together and top
  and bottom together, or each side on its own; corners are one radius or
  each corner its own; a shadow has colour, opacity, X, Y, blur and
  spread. Drag sideways on a field's icon to scrub its value.
- **Layers and preview**: the Layers bar selects or locks the reference,
  words, camera and logo; "Show as" previews the theme with a Bible
  verse, a song, a text or a speaker's name. Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z,
  Ctrl+Shift+Z) step back through every change.
- **Backgrounds**: a colour, gradient, picture (with blur and
  brightness), a live camera, or a looping video from your library.
- **Lower thirds**: a band at a set height, full width on the bottom edge
  by default, with margins from the frame's edges when you want it
  lifted or inset. Words placed freely in a band move with it.
- **Plates and borders**: a plate behind the words or the reference, with
  its own colour, image, corner radius and an optional border.
- **Tokens**: place the verse text, the reference, the translation tag,
  and the verse number as separate elements so they can be styled
  independently.
- **Verse numbers**: toggle visibility, render as superscript (with an
  adjustable size relative to the verse text), and give them their own
  colour — e.g. a yellow number in front of white verse text.

## Built-in starters

Litdeck ships with five opinionated starter themes (`src/lib/builtin-themes.ts`)
that you can clone and modify:

- **Classic Dark** — a high-contrast dark overlay tuned for typical
  stage lighting.
- **Modern Light** — a soft, light-on-dark variant for warmer venue
  palettes.
- **Lower Thirds** — a transparent-background band across the bottom of
  the frame, reference top-left with the verse text beneath it, for
  keying over a live camera feed.
- **Banner Split** — a free-positioning example with the reference in a
  top-left banner and the verse block detached below it.
- **Side by Side** — the camera in a rounded panel on the left and the
  words level with it on the right, over a background of your choice.
  **Swap sides** in the Camera tab puts the camera on the right.

**New** in the theme library also starts a theme from a layout: Full
Screen, Lower Thirds, **Custom** (the reference and words each in a box
you drag and resize anywhere) or Side by Side.

Each starter is a regular project file you can save, version, and
re-export.

## Switching themes live

![Broadcast settings for an output with its Theme menu open, listing the four built-in themes](/docs/screenshots/transport-theme-picker.png)

*Each output has its own theme. Open Broadcast outputs from the top bar, edit an output and pick a theme from the four built-ins (Classic Dark, Modern Light, Lower Thirds, Banner Split) or any you have made. **Click to expand.***


Themes can be activated from:

- The theme picker in the transport bar.
- A Stream Deck or other OSC controller (`/litdeck/theme`).
- The HTTP control endpoint (`POST /api/v1/command` with `{ "command": "theme", "value": "Classic Dark" }`).

The currently active theme is reflected in the **preview** panel before
it goes on-air, and committed to NDI when you toggle the on-air switch.

<Callout title="Resolution and color space" type="info">
NDI output is composited at the exact pixel dimensions of the canvas. If
your downstream switcher (vMix, OBS, Resolume) expects 1920×1080 with
straight alpha, set the canvas to that and Litdeck sends straight alpha
NDI frames natively.
</Callout>

## Saving and sharing

Themes are persisted to `broadcast-themes.json` in the app's data
directory via `tauri-plugin-store` (see `src/stores/broadcast-store.ts`),
so changes auto-save across restarts. The JSON is self-contained — no
external font files or absolute paths — so you can copy a theme between
machines or commit them to a service template repository for your team.
